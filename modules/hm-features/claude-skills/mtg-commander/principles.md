# Commander deckbuilding: what the creators agree on

Condensed from 1022 videos (October 2026): The Trinket Mage (Con) and the Magic Mirror
podcast he hosts with 3/3 Elk and Salubrious Snail; 3/3 Elk; Salubrious Snail; Maldhound;
and the Good Time Boys podcast on Decked Out EDH (VeggieWagon, Maldhound, Nerd Girl, Joe
Cherries). Transcripts were condensed into ~8000 claims, then merged by topic. A rule is
listed as consensus only when several of them back it independently; single-creator rules
are marked.

Read this before building, upgrading or critiquing a deck. It is advice, not law:
**Alex's house rules and the design brief in the skill override it** (Sol Ring is banned
even though Snail defends it; weird-and-functional beats optimal). Card names here were
verified against Scryfall when compiled; oracle text still comes from Scryfall, never
from this file. Links are timestamped to where the point is made.

## Building the deck

**One coherent plan; cut every card that doesn't push it toward a win.** Several plans
leave each with too few payoffs: "a mile wide and an inch deep", "multiple decks in a
trench coat". Openers end up with four cards of one plan and three of another, and engines
fight each other (Yawgmoth wrecks a go-wide army). Ask of each card "would I want this in
my opening hand?" Fulcrum cards should serve every part of the plan (Mayhem Devil hits both
creature and treasure sacrifice). Torn between two plans, build two decks. One theme plus
one sub-theme is clean. *All six; 107 claims.*
[Maldhound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VZ64W6Clqiw&t=1020s),
[Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Oh_a34vdtIA&t=61s)

**Fundamentals first; theme sits on top.** Never cut ramp, draw or removal for theme.
Find on-theme versions instead (Executioner's Capsule in artifacts). A "Theme" bucket
excuses weak cards. Precon upgrades start with the land base, which fixes most of the
problems. *All six.* [Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rM0384X69wM&t=370s)

**Give the deck a real way to close.** Engines without a conversion path (blink, artifact
value, group hug, planeswalker piles) drag games to 10+ turns of "senioritis", where the
outcome is decided but nobody can end it. A boring finisher beats none. A control deck needs
only a modest one, but it must exist. *All six.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ijZzWwEzooY&t=185s),
[Maldhound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=awKdkw10cfY&t=218s)

**Raise the floor before the ceiling.** Judge cards by their average game, not their best
one: "the #1 deckbuilding trap". Test: would it feel good hellbent and behind? Win-more is
dead in half to two-thirds of games. Filtering, cheap draw and cheap interaction are the
connective tissue that stop synergy decks whiffing; a deck that whiffs has a building
problem, not bad luck. *All six.* [Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6GzPtP7lq-4&t=183s)

**Don't hinge on the commander or any one piece.** If the commander is your best card it
always gets removed and games go feast-or-famine (Kaalia, Brago, Jodah). Keep functional
copies of key effects (Astral Slide works because Ephemerate and Soulherder copy it), and
backup plans that are another angle on the same strategy. Animar survives where Kaalia
doesn't because its 99 has draw and ramp to rebuild. *Snail, 3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage,
Maldhound; 70+ claims.* [Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=A4oASMevloo&t=303s)

**Set ratios from the commander, curve and testing, not a template.** The 37/10/10/10
template is wrong for most decks. Do the hypergeometric maths: 18 copies of an effect is
~77% to have one in the opener; 6 enablers plus 4 payoffs is only ~30% to have both in
15 cards. *3/3 Elk, Maldhound, Snail, Trinket Mage.*
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SLUV0TjK7LA&t=0s)

**EDHREC is a map, not a list.** It shows popularity, not fit, and it feeds back on itself.
Look early for roles, then close it before it biases you, and come back afterwards to check
what you missed. Scryfall oracle search finds what EDHREC can't: only ~5 of ~60,000 Jaxis
decks ran the Emrakul madness line. *All six.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vkC70bIXLw8&t=36s)

**Question staples; only Command Tower is automatic.** Muscle-memory staples never look
like cut candidates, so you never find the on-theme card. A decade of power creep has
outclassed many "staples": three-mana removal (Hero's Downfall, Murder) loses to two-mana
instants with riders (Bitter Triumph, Heartless Act); Fact or Fiction loses to Stock Up;
Burnished Hart loses to a Signet. "Merely fine" cards are the trap because you never see
what you're missing. Generic bombs flatten the deck: Rhystic Study made other control
engines pointless, and Craterhoof made Slimefoot's Saprolings irrelevant. *All six.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UAF5ZffX6gw&t=438s),
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fzMmnBNXU6s&t=245s)

**Make the commander fill one role and don't duplicate it in the 99.** Know whether it is
an enabler, a linchpin (the only route, so plan B is hard), an intensifier (speeds up a plan
that works without it), a payoff, or a counterweight that covers the deck's weakness
(Malfegor gives a discard deck the blockers it lacks; Massacre Girl adds a whole interaction pillar).
Counterweights are underused and give the most consistent decks. A payoff commander wants
enablers in the 99 and an enabler wants payoffs; a token commander needs payoffs, not more
token makers. *3/3 Elk, Snail, Trinket Mage, Maldhound.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jOCZGxIhgR8&t=120s),
[Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R8LqEt7k1-c&t=120s)

**Keep turns short.** Players check out after about two game actions a turn. One big
consolidating effect beats many small loops that each force a recheck (one Glimpse the
Unthinkable over a dozen drip-mill triggers); aim for about half a shuffle per turn. Avoid
tracking-heavy cards like Cathars' Crusade. *3/3 Elk, Maldhound, VeggieWagon, Nerd Girl.*
[Good Time Boys](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7fsCPoA99S4&t=616s)

**Keep the deck readable.** Decks that swing between fair midrange and instant combo make
threat assessment luck, and hidden wincons feel like robbery. A 10-second pregame pitch of
the plan and the strongest win is enough; if saying it ruins the deck, the deck was a
gimmick. *Snail, 3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage, Decked Out.*
[Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ccAea-2xqAU&t=338s)

**Goldfish turns 1-6, then play real games and find each loss's turning point.** Goldfishing
shows the floor; real games show what's clunky versus theoretical. Test against an equally
built opponent, because fairy-tale hands stop looking broken when the other side gets them
too (VeggieWagon). *3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage, VeggieWagon, Joe Cherries.*

Single-creator rules worth keeping:
- **Build to the opening hand** (Maldhound). Which cards make a good hand tells you whether
  the deck is combo, aggro, midrange or control.
- **Set a measurable goal**, e.g. "Scion of the Ur-Dragon on turn 3", and cut anything off the
  timeline (Maldhound).
- **Budget a deliberate ~5 jank slots** (Snail). Before the engine is online every card must
  work; after it, situational cards cost less.
- **Build pet cards around horizontal synergies** (3/3 Elk). In a vertical synergy the pet
  is the worst of its group; Shield Broker shines in flicker.
- **Watch for cards that quietly become the deck** (Maldhound). Monument to Endurance gets
  tutored over the wincon, and Village Bell-Ringer turns a Kiki-Jiki toolbox into pure combo.
- **Copy counts in a singleton 99** (VeggieWagon). 25+ for an effect wanted several times a
  game, 10-15 for once a game (finishers), fewer than 10 for skippable.

## Choosing the commander

**Pick one that asks something specific of the deck.** Kenrith, Urza, Atraxa and The
Ur-Dragon only ask for "good cards", so their decks converge on the same midrange pile and
play out the same way each time. Scion of the Ur-Dragon, or Garth One-Eye with its competing
asks, turns building into a puzzle. Five-colour and rule-bending commanders are best when
they restrict or reward the colours (Shalai cutting off a colour's core effects, Infinite
Guideline Station rewarding multicolour permanents). *3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage, Snail,
Maldhound, Decked Out.* [Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6w7BRYqD6bA&t=223s)

**Its text must work for the deck's real plan, from the command zone.** Tariel has no angel
synergy beyond Eminence and costs seven under tax. A fundamentally weak commander can't be
carried by the 99. Check the trigger wording against how the 99 actually plays: Nick Fury
wants a lone attacker, but flicker returns creatures tapped. *3/3 Elk, Maldhound, Decked
Out, Snail, Trinket Mage.* [3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0jqC23nhAiU&t=515s)

**Prefer commanders that make their own resources or profit from dying.** Draw on the
commander is a guaranteed extra card a turn and rebuilds after wipes; a two-mana looter let
a Jace deck run 29 lands. ETB and cast triggers turn removal into another trigger (Korvold,
Brago). *Snail, Maldhound, Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk.*

**Be wary of commanders that waste time, warp the game or dodge interaction.** Pure group
hug, Urza, Tergrid hand-locks, and tax-free casting (Yuriko, Derevi) frustrate tables;
visible costs (Liesa's life payment) feel fair. Removal and wipe commanders (Massacre Girl,
Kelsien) are like ten extra wipes, leave their own deck without a proactive plan, and strand
it when they die. *3/3 Elk, VeggieWagon, Snail, Trinket Mage, Maldhound.*

**Avoid the most popular commanders, or build them from a new angle** (Trinket Mage, Snail).
Con bans himself from EDHREC's top 100 and digs through older mono-coloured legends with
under ~1000 decks. This lines up with the design brief.

**Don't command a card that's better in the 99** (Lurrus, eight-drops that need
reanimation). Planeswalker commanders are fragile with three opponents' creatures free to
attack them. *Decked Out, 3/3 Elk, VeggieWagon, Snail, Maldhound.*

## Mana: lands and ramp

Source counts per colour come from the Karsten tables in the `mtg` skill. These rules
cover land count and ramp.

**Start high: 37-40 lands counting MDFCs, cyclers and utility lands, then tune.** Too few
lands is the most common brewing mistake. Lands are also spells now, so a high count costs
little. More lands let you mulligan for threats instead of mana and recover after sweepers.
Go lower (34-36) only with heavy cheap draw and filtering or 20+ ramp; go higher (40+) for
slow control and high curves, and 42+ for land-synergy decks. *Trinket Mage, Snail, 3/3 Elk,
VeggieWagon, Maldhound; 37 claims.* Dissent: VeggieWagon likes 32-35; Snail argues the free
mulligan supports much lower counts in low-curve decks.
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vZFT3-jdy0Q&t=1606s),
[Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nb_jLL3WrB4&t=123s)

**8-10 ramp as a baseline, sized to what the deck does.** That's critical mass to find one or
two early. Expensive central commanders, draw-heavy decks and combo with expensive tutors
want far more (18-20). Low curves, cheap-equipment Voltron and synergy decks want less. A
common mistake is adding rocks to a deck that isn't mana-hungry. *Trinket Mage, Snail, 3/3
Elk, Decked Out, Maldhound.* [Snail](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D2MRb3j4iYk&t=244s)

**Cheap ramp over 3-4 mana ramp.** Two two-mana ramp spells reach six mana a turn earlier
than one four-drop ramp spell. Turn 3 is too valuable for Cultivate outside a 1-3-5 ladder.
Untapped two-mana ramp (Nature's Lore, Three Visits) beats tapped (Rampant Growth, Farseek).
*Trinket Mage, Snail, 3/3 Elk, Maldhound, Joe Cherries.*

**Favour resilient ramp: land ramp and ETB-ramp creatures over rocks.** Land destruction is
taboo, so land ramp has no natural predator. Rocks die to Vandalblast and sweepers, and the
land player recovers a full turn faster after a wipe. Wood Elves effects survive removal,
trigger creature synergies and can be blinked. *Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, Snail, Maldhound.*
Rocks stay right in mana-hungry, artifact and colourless decks.
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QcEYhh-uJOs&t=246s)

**For big-mana targets (7+), run ramp that makes 2+ mana per card.** One-for-one rocks stall
short of eight. Step up to Thran Dynamo, Cradle-style lands or treasure makers, which pay for
themselves (Inspired Tinkering is effectively a two-mana draw three). *Maldhound, 3/3 Elk,
VeggieWagon, Snail, Trinket Mage.*

**Fix colours before adding ramp.** Pip failures derail more games than missing ramp: a
castable five-mana commander beats a seventh Cultivate. Chromatic Lantern is a trap outside
4-5 colours (fix the lands instead), and colourless utility lands like Temple of the False
God cost real colour. *Maldhound, 3/3 Elk, Snail, Trinket Mage.*

**Make every ramp piece lead somewhere** (Maldhound). Have payoffs worth reaching: ramping
past them is "ramping into nothing", and casting Cultivate 15 times doesn't win. Pair ramp
with a refill, because mana does nothing without cards to spend it on; climbing to 10+ mana
needs 5+ draw (Good Time Boys, VeggieWagon, Snail, Trinket Mage).

**Mulligan mana-light hands aggressively** (Trinket Mage, Snail). A high land count and a
card-drawing commander both make a six-card hand fine. Three lands plus a rock beats four
lands.

## Card advantage

**About 10+ real draw sources that net 2+ cards; cantrips don't count.** With three
opponents you need continuous access to cards or you fall into top-deck mode. Cantrips are
card-neutral and belong in storm, spellslinger and combo. *Trinket Mage, Snail, Maldhound,
Joey Schultz.* [Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FbAzZt_R3s8&t=270s)

**Count reuse, recursion and flicker as card advantage.** Recursion is "equally valuable" to
drawing, a flickered ETB tutor is several tutors, and engines that repeatedly make bodies are
as good as drawing. Cards that do two jobs stretch a slot. *All six.*

**Fit draw to the deck's structure.** Battlecruiser decks need mana sinks and X spells more
than redundant engines. Synergy draw (Morbid Opportunist next to removal, Darkstar Augur in
lifegain) beats generic draw, and a commander that draws means you need less. *Snail, 3/3
Elk, Good Time Boys, Trinket Mage.*

**Keep draw that works from behind.** Synergy-only, "optimistic" draw stops producing once
the payoffs are removed. Multi-card refills (Decree of Pain, Connecting the Dots) rebuild
after a wipe. *Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, Maldhound, Snail.*

**Repeatable engines with a good cards-per-mana rate beat one-shots over a long game**, but
judge them by payback speed. Phyrexian Arena cast on turn 3 breaks even around turn 7, when
bracket 3 games close (3/3 Elk). Engines without a once-per-turn clause are the most valuable
(Salvaging Station untaps on every death; Staff of Nin is once per turn). *Trinket Mage,
Maldhound, Good Time Boys, VeggieWagon; contested by 3/3 Elk.*

**Avoid symmetrical draw and wheels without payoffs or a restriction piece.** Wheel of
Fortune at one card in hand nets you +6 and the three opponents about +21. Never give
opponents both mana and cards (Prosperity), and with symmetric effects benefit first (Temple
Bell over Howling Mine). *Trinket Mage, Maldhound.*

**Make the land base generate value so flood hurts less:** MDFCs, channel lands, utility and
recursion lands, and scry density. *Trinket Mage, Snail, 3/3 Elk.*

## Interaction

**8-14 removal, scaled to how fast you win, plus 3-5 wipes for slower decks.** On average you
hold one answer when you need two. Slow, grindy decks must survive everything that
accumulates, so they need more; aggro still needs some. Precons average ~5 interaction
pieces, which is why their boards stall. *All six.* Dissent: VeggieWagon puts bracket 3 at
8-11; Snail counts 20+ total interaction for most decks.
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hFO1NulNhlU&t=33s)

**Keep it at 1-2 mana so you can hold it and still develop.** Develop first and answer second
and you stay ahead; tap out to answer and you're at parity. At three mana you either waste the
mana or fire at something you don't care about. *All six.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tlrIDmarGvM&t=343s)

**Answer every permanent type.** Many lists have no out to Rhystic Study or Smothering Tithe.
Universal answers (Beast Within, Generous Gift, Assassin's Trophy, Chaos Warp, Anguished
Unmaking) matter in casual because you don't know the pod. Own an answer to indestructible,
and carry one graveyard hate piece, slotted cheaply (Bojuka Bog, Soul-Guide Lantern). *Trinket
Mage, 3/3 Elk, Snail, Maldhound.*

**Aim removal at engines and bombs, not the first small body.** Each player casts 2-4
must-answer cards; a draw engine keeps someone alive for many turns, while a creature dealing
2 doesn't. A 10/10 hitting someone else is working for you. *All six.*

**Wipes should break parity, and be followed by a clock.** Unconditional symmetric wipes leave
you on an empty board while better players rebuild. One-sided or conditional sweepers (Winds of
Abandon, Martial Coup) progress you, and a wipe should be a pivot: develop, wipe, then deploy
the win. Include 1-2 artifact/enchantment wipes; they're criminally underplayed. *All six.*
[3/3 Elk](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aD2uz3YkfsU&t=152s)

**Make removal do a second job:** synergy (Shriekmaw and Accursed Marauder in recursion; Pile
On in tokens), modes, multiple targets, or MDFC lands. Repeatable removal engines (Salvaging
Station with Executioner's Capsule) beat piles of one-shots. *3/3 Elk, Snail, Trinket Mage,
Maldhound.*

**Treat stax and hate pieces as interaction, scaled to the table.** Soft taxes (Thorn of
Amethyst) fit mid power; hard shutoffs paralyse the table without advancing you. Keep cheap
answers to other people's locks (Nature's Claim, Force of Vigor). *All six.*

**Counterspells defend your win and stop pivotal spells; price them honestly.** As table
control they're a one-for-one against three opponents. Prefer counters with a second use for
held mana (Mystic Confluence, Cryptic Command). *Snail, Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, VeggieWagon.*

**Protect what the plan can't lose, and only that.** Voltron, toolbox and reanimator need
protection. Skip it where the commander is a supporting piece, because it lowers card quality.
Prefer protection with a second use (flicker, counters) over bare combat tricks. *3/3 Elk,
Maldhound, Trinket Mage, Snail.*

**Fogs must do something else** (Take the Bait, Blessed Respite, Orim's Chant). A bare fog buys
one turn. *3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage, Maldhound.*

## Win conditions and threats

**Never hang the win on one threat, one combo line or the commander alone.** If Craterhoof is
your only out, one counterspell ends you. Spread threats so opponents need a wipe, and keep a
backup kill. *Six creators.* [Maldhound](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SE6pg7KrKwg&t=1145s)

**Pick threats that scale or survive removal.** A cheap efficient body won't carry a 40-life,
three-opponent game. Good threats grow, generate resources, or shrug off removal (escape,
indestructible, ward). Prefer cards that bank value before removal lands (ETB, cast and death
triggers, haste): Dockside effectively doesn't die to removal, while Giant Adephage loses
huge tempo to a Murder. *Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, Maldhound, VeggieWagon.*
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fTW5Iuvk41w&t=31s)

**Re-price everything for 40 life, three opponents and 100 cards.** Two damage is 5% of a
life total, so Guide of Souls and Lava Spike shrink and plain burn aggro needs ~39 swings
from a Delver. Mill is tuned for 60 cards. Effects that scale with opponents grow: attackers
that hit each opponent do ~4x ("whenever a creature attacks a player alone" payoffs reward
spreading damage). *3/3 Elk, Trinket Mage, Snail, VeggieWagon, Maldhound.*

**Go-wide needs a real payoff to convert the board:** overrun, anthem, damage per token, or
sac outlets, and it needs to land fast. *3/3 Elk, Snail, Nerd Girl, Maldhound, Trinket Mage.*

**Prefer variable, on-theme finishers over one-card "I win" bombs** (Trinket Mage, Snail).
Definitive finishers (Craterhoof, Omniscience) make you the top-priority target and end games
before themes happen. Control running The Unspeakable instead of Blightsteel Colossus doesn't
force the table to go all in against it. The Trinket Mage also argues the opposite in other
videos, so treat this as the default, not a law.

**Combos are optional at casual power; if you run one, it should end the game quickly.**
Pieces should be good alone so the deck plays normally, the combo should be findable and
protected, and a backup combo should need setup. An obvious two-card combo pressures you to
go for it every game. *Maldhound, Snail, Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk.*

## Brackets and power

**Judge combos by speed, interaction windows and extra steps.** Two-card instant wins (Thassa's
Oracle plus Demonic Consultation) leave no window and belong at 3+ even in a weak deck. A
consistent four-piece combo is fine at 2-3, and multi-card jank combos end stalled bracket 2
games. *Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, Snail, Decked Out.*

**Rate a deck by its speed, its strongest repeatable play, and what it takes to answer it.**
If a wipe and ordinary removal answer you, lean bracket 2; if it takes specific answers,
bracket 3+. Game changers shouldn't consistently fire before turn 6 in bracket 3 or turn 8 in
bracket 2. One Armageddon in a 98-land deck is still MLD. *Trinket Mage, 3/3 Elk, Maldhound.*

**Brackets start the Rule Zero talk; they don't measure power.** A tuned bracket 2 deck beats
an untuned bracket 3 one, and adding game changers to a deck with bad ratios does nothing. Tell
the table about your combo up front. *Maldhound, Trinket Mage, Snail, 3/3 Elk, Decked Out.*

**Bracket 2 hell is real:** pillow forts with no enchantment removal, six wipes and no win con,
or everyone slow with no wipes. Keep bracket 2 slow, telegraphed and answerable, but still able
to close. *Trinket Mage, Snail.*

**Fast mana is the most common cause of one-sided games.** Cutting it made games happier
(Maldhound, Trinket Mage). Card-costing fast mana (Chrome Mox, Mox Diamond) is worse than two
ramp spells in long games.

**Watch raw-power engines that work in any deck** (Rhystic Study, Smothering Tithe,
Necropotence). They're good with nothing built around them, which is exactly what the game
changers list and salt track. *Trinket Mage, Maldhound, Snail.*

## At the table

**Apply early pressure and attack the greedy deck.** Not attacking enough is called the
biggest gameplay mistake. Engines that get going are close to unbeatable, pity slows games
around fourfold, and decisive targeting gives the table information. *Seven creators.*
[Trinket Mage](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NtXtKu_zVuI&t=268s)

**Politics is a free resource.** A deal to remove a problem permanent is worth about as much as
drawing cards, and it works around the colour pie. Keep deals short, explicit and honest, word
them by outcome not verb (Sunfall exiles, so it slips under a destroy-only deal), and never
break one. *Trinket Mage, VeggieWagon, Nerd Girl, Decked Out.*

**Rally the table against the leader with concrete numbers** ("30-card draw is 30 damage"),
not "that's scary". *Trinket Mage, Nerd Girl, VeggieWagon.*

**Sandbag until the first wipe is spent; keep your threat profile low.** Low-threat engines are
political in themselves (Tower of Fortunes over The Great Henge). *Trinket Mage, VeggieWagon.*
Snail and Maldhound disagree: visible power reads as fairer than a quiet setup that wins out of
nowhere.

**Don't lock the table out.** A stax piece or two is fine; a stax or stall deck costs you
games with friends. *Maldhound, Joe Cherries, 3/3 Elk, Snail.*

## Where they disagree

- **Tutors.** Trinket Mage: a second copy of your best card, fine anywhere. 3/3 Elk, Con and
  Maldhound: unrestricted tutors homogenise decks and make games repetitive. Shared ground:
  a tutor is only as good, or as fun, as its targets, and "always tutors the same card" is a
  variance spike to cut.
- **Sol Ring.** Maldhound and Trinket Mage cut it for fairer games; Snail calls it a cheap
  equaliser that fades after turn 3-4. House rule: banned.
- **Land count.** 37 (Trinket Mage minimum) to 40 (3/3 Elk) versus 32-35 (VeggieWagon). It
  resolves on ramp and draw density.
- **Engines vs one-shot draw** (above). Phyrexian Arena is the battleground.
- **Combos at casual tables.** 3/3 Elk: unnecessary at 2-3, and bolted-on pieces become dead
  draws. Trinket Mage: they suit mid power because games end and nobody sits out.
- **Arcane Denial.** Trinket Mage: F tier, it gifts a card. 3/3 Elk: fine in combo
  protecting one turn.
- **Hiding vs showing threats** (above).

## Card verdicts with reasons

Ramp and lands:
- **Arcane Signet**: efficient, but not automatic; only if you use the mana.
- **Cultivate**: filler outside a 1-3-5 curve; fixing matters more than a seventh copy.
- **Chromatic Lantern**: a trap outside 4-5 colours.
- **Walking Atlas**, **Sand Scout**, **Loyal Warhound**, **Dreamscape Artist**: underplayed
  ramp; Sand Scout and Warhound are catch-up plays that work even on the play.
- **Reliquary Tower**: belongs in under 5% of decks, and hurts discard decks.
- **Temple of the False God**: a liability without ~40 lands; never count it for a mulligan.
- **Myriad Landscape**: only at 39-41 lands.
- **Evolving Wilds**, **Terramorphic Expanse**: overplayed outside landfall and Deserts.

Draw:
- **Rhystic Study**: generically strong, salty, and it flattens the other 98 cards. Several
  creators cut it.
- **Phyrexian Arena**: contested; slow payback, and a dead topdeck late.
- **Night's Whisper**, **Stock Up**, **Nasty End**: cheap draw with a high floor.
- **Skullclamp**: any 1/1 becomes a one-mana draw two.
- **Salvaging Station** over **Staff of Nin**: it untaps on every death.
- **Brainstorm**: overplayed; needs shuffles or many-spell turns.
- **Wheel of Fortune**, **Windfall**: refuel opponents; cEDH or dedicated wheels only.
- **Mystic Remora**: cumulative upkeep means playing less Magic.

Removal and wipes:
- **Assassin's Trophy**, **Beast Within**, **Generous Gift**, **Chaos Warp**: hit anything.
- **Swords to Plowshares**: great but creature-only; any-permanent answers matter more in casual.
- **Parting Gust**: also protection and flicker; displacing Swords in flicker decks.
- **Leadership Vacuum**: absurdly efficient against commanders.
- **Hero's Downfall**, **Murder**: outclassed.
- **Farewell**: great when behind, bad when ahead.
- **Cyclonic Rift**: just a wipe unless you have fliers or bounce synergy.
- **Doomskar**, **Kaya's Wrath**, **Oversimplify**: strong budget wipes.

Counters and protection:
- **Counterspell**: A tier. **Fierce Guardianship**, **Force of Negation**: S tier.
- **Negate**: misses creatures. **Force of Will**: poor in casual.
- **Mystic Confluence**, **Cryptic Command**: worth five because they can just draw.
- **Teferi's Protection**: the best white protection, and as agency-denying as stax.
- **Lightning Greaves**, **Swiftfoot Boots**: contested; Greaves rates about 5/10 outside
  haste or tap synergy.

Tutors:
- **Demonic Tutor**, **Vampiric Tutor**: only as strong as the targets; often jammed without
  checking the deck uses them.
- **Natural Order**: always Craterhoof, so keep it out of bracket 2.

Overrated amplifiers and payoffs:
- **Doubling Season**: win-more; Hardened Scales is cheaper, and an anthem can do more on 1/1s.
- **Panharmonicon**: ETB decks only. **Parallel Lives**, **Anointed Procession**: need many
  token sources.
- **Blood Artist**: the most overrated aristocrats card (VeggieWagon); one per sac outlet isn't
  enough redundancy.
- **Helm of the Host**: nine mana and a Path target.
- **Thousand-Year Storm**, **Mizzix's Mastery**: only where they win outright.

Threats and engines:
- **Craterhoof Behemoth**: the most direct parity breaker, but the least interesting, and a
  single point of failure.
- **Moonshaker Cavalry**: a flying overrun, sometimes better than Craterhoof.
- **Thassa's Oracle**: contested; harmless without a shell.
- **The One Ring**: colourless, needs no support, scales late.
- **Eternal Witness** beats **Regrowth**: ETB creatures beat the equivalent sorcery.
- **Giant Adephage**: dies to Murder for nothing; **Two-Headed Hellkite** draws first.
- **Sun Titan**: misses typeless lands but aged well. **Primeval Titan**: a design mistake.

Politics:
- **Bladegriff Prototype**, **Hunted Horror**: good political cards; opponents choose.
- **Tower of Fortunes**: draws less removal than The Great Henge.
- **Expropriate**, **Temporal Trespass**: votes follow perceived advantage, so they're miserable
  play patterns.
